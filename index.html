How it works

Most home construction sites that disturb an acre or more, or are part of a larger subdivision, are covered by a construction stormwater permit and need a stormwater pollution prevention plan, or SWPPP. The permit usually requires regular inspections — often weekly — and inspections after rain above a set amount, with written reports and corrective actions for every problem found. Missed inspections are one of the most common findings when a state or city inspector visits.

For a small builder the problem is not the inspection itself — it takes fifteen minutes — but remembering it every week on every lot, noticing that it rained enough to trigger an extra inspection, and keeping the reports in order. SWPPP Inspector takes care of the reminders and the paperwork.

Each site is set up once with its permit, the inspection frequency and the rain trigger. The service tracks rainfall at the site location and sends an alert when an after-rain inspection is due, along with the weekly reminder. The site super opens the checklist on the phone.

The checklist follows the controls in your plan: perimeter controls such as silt fence, inlet protection, the stabilized construction entrance, stockpiles, concrete washout, and stabilization of finished areas. Each item gets a quick answer and a photo. Problems become corrective actions with a deadline; when they are fixed, the super adds a photo and the action closes.

When the inspection is complete, the report is generated with the date, the inspector, the rain data, the findings and the photos, and is signed on the phone. Reports are stored by site and permit, ready when a state or city inspector asks, and ready for the notice of termination when the lots are stabilized.

The office sees all sites on one screen: inspections done, due and overdue, and open corrective actions. It is easy to show a lender, a city or a buyer that the site was managed properly.

Setting up takes minutes per site: address, permit number, inspection frequency, rain trigger and the people who inspect.

On a busy week with several lots, the super does not need to remember which site is due. The morning list shows which inspections are due today, which corrective actions are still open and which sites had rain overnight. Each inspection takes the same few minutes on the phone, and nothing is written twice.

The service supports inspections and keeps records; the permittee remains responsible for permit compliance and for the content of the reports. We are not affiliated with EPA or any state or local stormwater agency.

Questions

How do rain alerts work?

Rainfall is tracked for each site location; when it reaches the trigger in your permit, you get an alert that an inspection is due.

Can I use my own SWPPP checklist?

Yes. The checklist follows the controls in your plan and can be adjusted per site.

Are reports signed?

Yes, on the phone by the person who inspected.

Is this for large developers?

It is built for small home builders with one or several sites.

Can I use it already?

Not yet. The service is in preparation, and we are checking whether builders need it.
